About Hands Against Child Marriage

Our full story, approach, and partnerships

Hands Against Child Marriage (HACM) is a dedicated youth-led movement and grassroots organisation committed to ending child marriage and safeguarding the rights and dignity of children in Uganda. Our work contributes directly to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly those focused on gender equality, quality education, reduced inequalities, and child protection.

The organisation was founded by Namawejje Hajarah, Magumbye Felix, and Mabala Emma, driven by a deep belief in social justice, community empowerment, and the protection of children from harmful practices. The founders bring a powerful combination of professional experience and lived realities to the movement. Having worked with Mutera Global Healing, a renowned organisation dedicated to eradicating childhood sexual violence in Rwanda and Uganda, the team draws on both technical expertise and personal stories of resilience, healing, and hope.

HACM is grounded in the principles of social justice and believes that sustainable change begins with informed and empowered communities. The organisation believes true social change can only happen when communities especially young people are educated and empowered to think deeply about their future selves and understand how to create systems that lead to a vision free from child marriage.

That is why we work with girls, boys, young people, parents, and community leaders to challenge the social norms, traditions, and systems that perpetuate child marriage. A key pillar of our work is engaging men and boys as allies, recognising their critical role in preventing child marriage and promoting gender equality.

Our Partnerships

  • Schools and educational institutions across Bugiri District
  • Civil Society Organisations (CSOs) and Faith-Based Organisations (FBOs)
  • Cultural institutions and government Ministries, Departments & Agencies (MDAs)
  • Grassroots organisations and community-based structures
  • Future Rights providing mentoring, capacity-building, and funding access

Our Core Values

The principles that guide everything we do

Social Justice

HACM is a non-discriminative organisation that ensures diversity is realised at all times. We advocate for fairness, equality, and dignity for all people, including persons with disabilities.

Empowerment

Through our programmes, we empower children, adolescents, young people, parents, and caregivers with hands-on entrepreneurial skills, soft skills, and leadership development skills to fight against poverty and its associated effects that lead to child marriage.

Accountability

HACM takes full responsibility for its work and remains accountable and answerable to community stakeholders, donors, and partners during its existence and implementation.

Transparency

We operate with openness and honesty, ensuring that our decisions and actions are clear to all community stakeholders and partners.

Inclusivity

We champion an environment where every person, regardless of background, gender, race, or ability, is valued, respected, and actively included in decision-making and the design of our initiatives.

I Belong to School

School & community-based child protection and education initiative

The I Belong to School Programme is a school and community-based child protection and education initiative implemented by Hands Against Child Marriage in primary and secondary schools. This programme responds to the strong and well-documented link between school dropout, child marriage, early pregnancy, and child labour.

In Uganda, approximately 1 in 3 girls is married before the age of 18, and about 25% of girls aged 15–19 have begun childbearing. At the same time, nearly a quarter of children are engaged in child labour. These challenges are most severe in rural districts, where poverty, harmful gender norms, sexual violence, and limited access to support services continue to push children out of school.

What the Programme Does

  • Conducts age-appropriate awareness sessions on personal safety, respect, and healthy relationships
  • Promotes school re-enrolment and provides school fees support with scholastic materials
  • Offers psychosocial support and child empowerment sessions for healing and confidence-building
  • Engages parents, caregivers, teachers, and local leaders through dialogues and community barazas
  • Uses local radio outreach to challenge harmful norms around child marriage
  • Strengthens referral and response systems for children at risk

Impact

By combining education support with community action, the programme increases school enrolment and retention, reduces risks of child marriage and early pregnancy, and positions education as a protective pathway ensuring every child can remain in school and grow toward a safe, dignified, and hopeful future.

Trauma Healing & Child Protection Workshops

Workshops and seminars for communities and child protection stake holders

HACM conducts trauma healing and child protection workshops and seminars to support the wellbeing and safety of children and communities affected by child marriage and related forms of violence. These workshops are conducted upon invitation by schools, community institutions, organisations, and local structures that seek to strengthen child protection and promote healing.

Our Approach

The workshops and seminars are facilitated by professional counsellors and survivor advocates, drawing from HACM's strong grounding in trauma-informed practice. The approach is shaped by the lived and professional experiences of HACM's founders, who are survivor advocates and trauma healing facilitators with experience from Mutera Global Healing a renowned organisation ending childhood sexual violence in Uganda and Rwanda.

Key Features

  • Offered at no cost to institutions and organisations
  • Facilitated by trained counsellors and survivor advocates
  • Practical, respectful sessions rooted in real community realities
  • Creates safe spaces for learning, reflection, and healing
  • Involves the entire child protection system and community stakeholders

By providing free, accessible trauma healing and child protection support, HACM aims to strengthen community capacity, reduce harm, and contribute to safer environments where children can heal, remain protected, and pursue their education with dignity.

A Birthday for a Cause

Transforming personal celebrations into acts of solidarity and inspiration

Birthday for a Cause is a community-centred activity by Hands Against Child Marriage that transforms personal celebrations into moments of care, connection, and protection for children. Through this initiative, HACM staff and key supporters celebrate their birthdays with children in selected communities, creating joyful and meaningful experiences that centre children's wellbeing.

What Happens

  • Each event is prepared and coordinated by HACM and brings together children, caregivers, staff, and partners
  • Activities include games, celebrating growth, sharing motivation, and quality time together
  • Child-friendly sessions on body autonomy, safety, and protection are held within the warm setting
  • Support items raised through prior fundraising campaigns are distributed to families and children

How to Get Involved

Partners, well-wishers, and child advocates are encouraged to join the team whether by fundraising, participating in the celebration, or supporting delivery so that every birthday becomes a meaningful act of solidarity and hope for children.

Learning, Networking & Knowledge Exchange

Capacity-building for staff and child protection professionals

The Learning, Networking, and Knowledge Exchange programme at HACM is designed to strengthen the skills, knowledge, and collaboration of its staff and partners in child protection. Through this programme, HACM keeps its doors open to invitations from other organisations for workshops, conferences, webinars, and other educational platforms.

Programme Structure

  • HACM participates in external workshops, conferences, and webinars to gain new insights
  • Professionals, government officials, and child protection officers conduct in-house trainings twice a year
  • Online sessions and discussions on current child protection challenges are hosted and attended
  • Resources, tools, and innovative approaches are shared across organisations

Our Philosophy

The programme encourages continuous learning, unlearning, and relearning ensuring that both HACM staff and participating professionals remain equipped and updated to respond effectively to the evolving needs of children and communities while ensuring best practices in child protection.
